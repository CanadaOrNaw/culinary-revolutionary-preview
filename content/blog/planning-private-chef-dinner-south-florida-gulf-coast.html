1. Start with the occasion and the pace of the evening

An anniversary dinner, a birthday with friends and a relaxed family gathering call for different plans. Before choosing dishes, decide what matters most: time around the table, a sequence of individually plated courses, or a more informal meal with room for conversation.

Share your approximate guest count, preferred date and ideal serving time. If children are joining, or some guests need to leave earlier, mention that too. Those details help shape the number of courses and the service flow. You do not need to have every answer before making an inquiry.

2. Use a sample menu as a starting point

A sample menu makes it easier to explain what you like. Culinary Revolutionary’s seven sample menus span Italian, brunch, Spanish, Key West, wild game, tapas and vegetarian options. They are starting points for a conversation, not a requirement to order an entire menu unchanged.

  • Italian: a useful direction when you want to explore a multi-course dinner built around familiar flavors.
  • Spanish tapas: worth discussing when smaller dishes and a sociable pace appeal to your group.
  • Key West: a place to look for coastal menu inspiration.
  • Vegetarian: a dedicated starting point for a vegetable-focused meal, rather than treating it as an afterthought.

Pick a few dishes or a cuisine you enjoy, then ask which combinations work for your group and kitchen. Confirm ingredient availability, portions and course selections with the chef before treating any sample as your final event menu.

3. Bring dietary needs into the first conversation

Ask guests about food allergies, dietary requirements and ingredients they prefer to avoid before the menu is finalized. Pass along specific information instead of a general note such as “a few restrictions.” A preference and a food allergy may require different planning.

Let the chef know if several guests need the same adjustment or if one person needs a separate dish. For an allergy, discuss ingredients, preparation and the limitations of the event kitchen directly with the chef. Do not assume that a sample-menu label guarantees an allergen-free environment.

4. Describe the kitchen and dining space

The chef needs to plan for the kitchen that is actually available. A short description of the oven, stovetop, refrigerator space and usable work surfaces is a helpful start. Photos can make the conversation easier when you are arranging dinner at a property you do not know well.

If you are staying in a rental home or using a shared event space, check the property’s rules for outside chefs, guests and access. Confirm arrival instructions, parking and any stairs or building-access requirements. For an outdoor meal, agree on an indoor alternative in case the weather changes.

Also ask who will provide plates, glasses, flatware, linens and any additional service staff. Agree on what setup and cleanup include before the event so that the end of the evening is as clear as the beginning.

5. Confirm the scope in a written quote

A useful quote should tell you what you are booking, not just provide a total. Ask for the agreed menu, guest count, service timing and any staffing or equipment arrangements. Discuss your budget early so the proposal can be built around it.

Clarify whether the quote includes ingredients, travel, rentals and cleanup, and whether any taxes or other charges apply. Ask about the booking deposit, payment schedule, cancellation terms and the deadline for changes to the guest count. These details vary by event; a sample menu is not a price or a confirmed reservation.

A few common planning questions

Do I need to choose a complete menu before contacting the chef?

No. A cuisine, a few favorite dishes or a description of the occasion gives the conversation a starting point. You can browse the sample menus first or explain what you have in mind in your inquiry.

Can I arrange dinner in a vacation rental?

Ask the property owner or manager whether outside chef services are allowed, then share the location and kitchen details. The chef will need to confirm that the space and travel arrangements work for your event.
